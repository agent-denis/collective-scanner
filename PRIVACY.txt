PRIVACY POLICY - COLLECTIVE SCANNER
====================================

Last updated: 16 November 2026
Applies to Collective Scanner version 1.0.0 and later

Collective Scanner is a free, open-source program (GNU GPL v3.0 or later)
that runs entirely on your own computer. This policy explains what
information it handles and what it does with it.


1. SUMMARY
----------

  * No data collection. Collective Scanner does not collect, store, sell or
    share any personal data. The author receives nothing.
  * No accounts, analytics, advertising, telemetry or crash reporting.
  * Everything stays on your computer. The dashboard is served only on
    127.0.0.1 (localhost), so other devices cannot open it.
  * No third-party resources. The page loads no external fonts, scripts,
    images or trackers.


2. WHAT INFORMATION THE PROGRAM READS
-------------------------------------

To show its dashboard, the program reads information LOCALLY from your
computer, while it is running:

  System
    Processor, memory, storage, temperature and battery levels; names,
    owners and resource use of running processes.
    Source: /proc, /sys

  Disk
    Drive sizes; names and sizes of files and folders you choose to explore.
    Source: the file system

  Wi-Fi
    Names (SSIDs), hardware addresses, signal strength and security type of
    nearby wireless networks.
    Source: NetworkManager (nmcli)

  Bluetooth
    Names, addresses, type and signal strength of nearby Bluetooth devices.
    Source: BlueZ (bluetoothctl, D-Bus)

  Network
    Your IP address, router, DNS servers, link speed; addresses, names and
    makers of devices on your home network.
    Source: ip, nmcli, ping, avahi-resolve

  Traffic
    Network connections of YOUR OWN USER'S programs: program name, remote
    address and port, and the number of bytes sent and received.
    Source: ss, /proc/net/dev

The content of your network traffic is never read, recorded or inspected.
Only connection details and byte counts are used.

This information is held in memory only while the program runs and is gone
when it stops. Nothing is written to disk, except files you choose to export
(see section 4).


3. NETWORK ACTIVITY PERFORMED BY THE PROGRAM
--------------------------------------------

Collective Scanner does not contact the author or any server run by the
author. It does make the following network requests in order to work:

  1. Connectivity check (Network section and Overview, about once a minute
     while they are open): one ping to your router, one ping to 1.1.1.1
     (Cloudflare's public DNS service), and one DNS lookup of
     fedoraproject.org. These reveal your public IP address to those
     services, as any normal internet use does.

  2. Reverse-DNS lookups (Traffic section): the addresses your programs
     connect to are looked up through your computer's configured DNS
     server, so that names such as "google.com" can be shown instead of
     numbers.

  3. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth scans: your adapters listen for, and may briefly
     announce themselves to, nearby devices. This is the same as opening
     your system's Wi-Fi or Bluetooth settings. The program never connects
     or pairs.

  4. Home-network scan (only when you press "Find devices"): sends one ping
     to each address on your local network.

  5. Port check (only when you press "Start check"): connects to the ports
     of the device you enter, and may read the short greeting a service
     sends back.

Only scan networks and devices that you own or have permission to test.


4. INFORMATION STORED ON YOUR COMPUTER
--------------------------------------

  * Browser storage: the page remembers your theme choice (light or dark)
    and the last section you opened, in your browser's local storage. This
    never leaves your browser. You can clear it with your browser's "clear
    site data" option.

  * Exports: CSV and PDF files are created only when you click Export, and
    are saved where you choose (normally your Downloads folder). They may
    contain the information listed above, such as addresses, device names
    and program names. Treat them as private and take care when sharing
    them.


5. CHILDREN
-----------

The program collects no data from anyone, including children.
